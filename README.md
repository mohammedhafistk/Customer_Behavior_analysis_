# Customer_Behavior_analysis_
End-to-end customer shopping behavior analysis using Python, SQL, and Power BI — data cleaning &amp; feature engineering in Pandas, 10 business-question SQL queries  and an interactive Power BI dashboard.

# 🎯 Objective

- Analyze customer shopping behavior across 3,900 retail transactions to understand what drives revenue, retention, and customer satisfaction.
- Clean and prepare raw transactional data for reliable analysis.
- Answer specific business questions using SQL to surface actionable patterns.
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard so non-technical stakeholders can explore the same insights without writing code.
- Translate findings into concrete business recommendations around subscriptions, discounting, and customer retention.

## ❓ KPI / Business Questions

1. What is the total revenue generated by male vs. female customers?
2. Which customers used a discount but still spent more than the average purchase amount?
3. Which are the top 5 products with the highest average review rating?
4. How do average purchase amounts compare between Standard and Express shipping?
5. Do subscribed customers spend more than non-subscribers?
6. Which 5 products have the highest percentage of purchases made with a discount?
7. How many customers fall into New, Returning, and Loyal segments?
8. What are the top 3 most purchased products within each category?
9. Are repeat buyers (5+ previous purchases) more likely to be subscribers?
10. What is the revenue contribution of each age group?

## 🔄 Process

### 1. Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering (Python)
- Loaded and inspected the raw dataset (3,900 rows × 18 columns) using `pandas`.
- Filled 37 missing `Review Rating` values using the median rating per product category.
- Standardized column names to snake_case.
- Engineered `age_group` (quartile-based binning) and `purchase_frequency_days` (converted text frequency to numeric days).
- Detected and removed a redundant column (`promo_code_used` was identical to `discount_applied`).
- Exported the cleaned dataset and loaded it into PostgreSQL.

### 2. Business Analysis (SQL)
- Answered the 10 questions above using `GROUP BY` aggregations, a correlated subquery, CTEs, and a `ROW_NUMBER()` window function.

### 3. Visualization (Power BI)
- Built a single-page interactive dashboard with KPI cards, a subscription-status donut chart, category and age-group breakdowns, and slicers for gender, category, shipping type, and subscription status.

## 💡 Insights

- **Gender gap:** Male customers generated $157,890 in revenue vs. $75,191 from female customers — nearly double.
- **Discount ≠ bargain hunting:** 839 customers used a discount and still spent above the dataset average.
- **Shipping speed barely matters:** Express orders averaged only ~$2 more than Standard ($60.48 vs. $58.46).
- **Subscription isn't lifting spend:** Subscribers and non-subscribers spend almost the same per order (~$59–60); non-subscribers only generate more total revenue because there are ~3x more of them.
- **Strong loyalty, weak acquisition:** 80% of customers are "Loyal" (10+ purchases), but only 2% are brand-new — retention is solid, growth needs new customers.
- **Category leaders:** Pants, Blouse, and Jewelry are the top sellers in their categories; Gloves, Sandals, and Boots have the highest review ratings.
- **Age group revenue:** Fairly even across groups, with Young Adults slightly ahead ($62,143).

## ✅ Conclusion

This project demonstrates a complete analytics workflow — from raw, messy data to a polished, interactive dashboard. Python handled data quality and feature engineering, SQL translated business questions into precise and reproducible queries, and Power BI made the findings accessible to non-technical stakeholders. The analysis points to two clear opportunities: reworking the subscription program's value proposition (since it isn't currently driving higher spend) and investing more in new-customer acquisition (since retention is already strong).
